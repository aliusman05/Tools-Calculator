## Our approach

- **Accuracy.** Each calculator's math lives in plain, separately tested code. We compare the results with independently calculated reference values, such as published payment tables and closed-form formulas, and we test edge cases like zero interest, very short and very long terms, large amounts and invalid input.
- **Transparency.** Formulas, assumptions and "last updated" dates are shown on every page. Where a calculator relies on official figures, such as US federal tax brackets, they are kept in one dated data file with a link to the source.
- **Privacy.** Calculations run in your browser. The numbers you type are not sent to our servers. Read the [privacy policy](/privacy-policy) for the details.
- **Original explanations.** We write our own explanations and examples. We do not copy text from other sites.
- **Independence.** The site is supported by advertising and, in places, affiliate links, which are always labeled. They do not change how a calculator works or what it tells you.

## How we check the formulas

1. We write the formula down in plain terms and note the source or derivation.
2. We implement it as a pure function with no interface code, so it can be tested on its own.
3. We test it against hand-calculated or independently derived values, and with properties that must always hold. For example, a higher interest rate should never lower a loan payment, and a loan schedule must finish at exactly zero with the principal repaid equal to the amount borrowed.
4. We write the page content around a worked example that is checked against the live calculator.

## Keeping things up to date

Tax figures and similar data are reviewed when official numbers are published each year. Each calculator shows the date it was last reviewed. If you find something that looks wrong or out of date, please let us know.
