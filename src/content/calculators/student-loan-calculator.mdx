Use this student loan calculator to see how fast you could be debt-free. Enter what you owe, the interest rate and the term, then add an extra amount each month or a one-time lump sum, such as a bonus or a tax refund. You will get your payoff date, the total interest you would pay, and exactly how much time and interest the extra money saves compared with paying only the scheduled amount.

## How to use this calculator

1. Enter the **balance to repay**. Use the amount your servicer shows as owed, including any interest that has been added to it.
2. Enter the **interest rate** and the **repayment term** left on the loan.
3. Set the month of your **first payment** so the schedule shows real dates.
4. Add an **extra monthly amount**, a **lump sum**, or both. For the lump sum, type the payment number it goes with: 1 is your first payment, 12 is your twelfth.
5. Compare the results with and without your extras. The summary shows the interest saved and the months removed.

## How it works

The scheduled payment for a fixed-rate loan is the same every month:

```
M = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)
```

- **P** is the balance, **r** is the monthly rate (the annual rate divided by 12 and by 100) and **n** is the number of monthly payments.
- Each month, interest is the current balance times r. The rest of the payment reduces the balance.
- Extra money is added to principal in the month it is paid. A smaller balance means less interest next month, so each extra dollar keeps working for the rest of the loan.
- A lump sum is applied once, in the payment you choose, and your scheduled payment stays the same afterward. The loan simply ends sooner.

The comparison runs the same loan twice, once with only the scheduled payment and once with your extras, and shows the difference.

## Worked example

You owe **$35,000** at **5.5%** with **10 years** to go.

- The monthly payment is **$379.84**. In the first month, **$160.42** is interest and **$219.43** reduces the balance.
- Over 120 payments you pay **$10,581.04** in interest, **$45,581.04** in total.

Now add extra money:

- **$150 extra every month:** done in 79 payments (6 years 7 months), with **$6,785.14** of interest. That saves **$3,795.89** and 41 months.
- **A $5,000 lump sum with payment 12:** done in 100 payments (8 years 4 months), with **$7,750.27** of interest. That saves **$2,830.76** and 20 months.
- **Both together:** done in 67 payments (5 years 7 months), with **$5,173.76** of interest. That saves **$5,407.28** and 53 months.

Early payments send a large share to interest ($160.42 of the first $379.84), which is why a lump sum paid early saves so much: it removes principal while the balance is still large.

## What this calculator does not cover

- **Income-driven repayment, forgiveness and other federal programs.** These exist and can change what you pay, but they follow rules that change over time and depend on your loans and income. This calculator does not model them. For the current rules, see the official source at [studentaid.gov](https://studentaid.gov/).
- **Deferment, forbearance and grace periods.** Interest may be added to your balance during these times. Enter the balance you owe when repayment starts, after any such interest is included, and the schedule from there is correct.
- **Variable rates and rate changes.** The rate you enter is used for the whole loan. If your rate can change, run a higher rate as a test.
- **Fees, taxes and credits.** Origination fees, interest rate discounts for automatic payments and any tax benefit are not included.

For several loans at once, the [debt payoff planner](/debt-payoff-planner) compares different payoff orders. For a simple loan that is not a student loan, use the [loan calculator](/loan-calculator).

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Pay extra on purpose.** Tell your servicer to apply the extra to principal, and check the balance on your next statement.
- **Start with the highest rate** if you have several loans, after you have covered every minimum payment.
- **Keep a cushion.** Money paid to a loan cannot be taken back, so keep some savings for emergencies before you send extra.
- **Re-run it when things change.** A raise, a bonus or a new rate is a good reason to try the numbers again.
- **Check your servicer's balance.** The figure on your latest statement is the one to use, not the amount you originally borrowed.
