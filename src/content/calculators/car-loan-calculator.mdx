A car loan looks simple until taxes, fees, a trade-in and a down payment all enter the picture. This calculator works out the amount you will actually finance, your monthly payment, the total interest and the full cost of the car. Use it before you visit a dealership to set a budget, or afterward to check an offer.

<Calculator />

## How to use this calculator

1. Enter the **vehicle price** and your **down payment**.
2. Add your **trade-in value**, and the amount you **still owe** on that car, if any.
3. Enter your local **sales tax** and choose whether the trade-in is subtracted before tax is applied.
4. Add **title, registration and other fees**.
5. Enter the **interest rate** and **loan term** in months, then compare the payment with your monthly budget.

## How it works

The amount financed is:

```
Financed = Price + Sales tax + Fees − Down payment − Trade-in value + Amount owed on trade-in
```

Sales tax is the taxable amount times your rate. The taxable amount is the price, or the price minus the trade-in value if your state works that way. The monthly payment then comes from the standard loan formula:

```
M = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)
```

where **P** is the amount financed, **r** is the monthly interest rate and **n** is the number of months. At a 0% rate it is simply P ÷ n. The out-the-door price is the price plus tax plus fees, and the total cost of the car adds the interest you pay.

## Worked example

You buy a **$32,000** car with **$4,000** down and a **$3,000** trade-in. Sales tax is **7%** applied after the trade-in, fees are **$500**, and you finance at **6.5%** for **60 months**.

- Sales tax: ($32,000 − $3,000) × 7% = **$2,030**.
- Out-the-door price: **$34,530**.
- Amount financed: $34,530 − $4,000 − $3,000 = **$27,530**.
- Monthly payment: **$538.66**.
- Total interest over five years: **$4,789.36**, so the car costs about **$39,319** in all.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Negotiate the out-the-door price, not the payment.** A low payment can hide a long term or extra fees.
- **Get pre-approved.** A rate from your bank or credit union gives you something to compare with the dealer's offer.
- **Watch the term.** Terms of 72 months or more raise the interest cost and the chance of owing more than the car is worth.
- **Do not forget insurance and fuel.** They are not part of the loan but they are part of the monthly cost of owning a car.
- **Check your state's tax rule.** Local taxes can differ from the state rate, so use the total you expect to pay.
