Credit card debt can feel endless because the interest keeps arriving every month. This calculator shows exactly how long it will take to clear a card at your chosen payment, how much interest you will pay, and, in the other direction, what monthly payment you need to be debt-free by a particular date. It also warns you if a payment is too small to make progress.

## How to use this calculator

1. Choose whether you want to know **how long it takes** at a payment, or **what to pay** to finish within a number of months.
2. Enter your current **balance** and the card's **APR**.
3. Enter your **monthly payment** or your **target number of months**.
4. Read the payoff time, total interest and debt-free date.
5. Try raising the payment a little to see how much time and interest you save.

## How it works

Each month the card adds interest equal to your balance times the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and then your payment is taken off:

```
New balance = Balance × (1 + monthly rate) − Payment
```

This repeats until the balance reaches zero, and the last payment is whatever is left. The number of months can also be found directly:

```
n = −ln(1 − Balance × r / Payment) / ln(1 + r)
```

This only works when the payment is larger than **Balance × r**, which is the first month's interest. For a target date, the payment needed is **Balance × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)**.

## Worked example

You owe **$5,000** on a card with a **20%** APR, so the first month's interest is **$83.33**.

- Paying **$200 a month** clears the card in **33 months** with **$1,522.10** of interest.
- To be debt-free in **24 months**, you need to pay **$254.48** a month, and the interest falls to **$1,107.50**.
- Paying only **$100 a month** takes **109 months**, over nine years, and costs **$5,840.10** in interest, more than the original balance.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Pay more than the minimum.** Even a modest increase can cut years off the payoff.
- **Stop adding new charges.** A balance that keeps growing cannot be cleared by a fixed payment.
- **Target the highest APR first** if you have several cards. The debt payoff planner compares strategies for you.
- **Check for a lower rate.** A balance transfer or a lower-rate personal loan can reduce interest, but watch the fees.
- **Use windfalls.** A bonus or tax refund applied to the balance can shorten the payoff substantially.
