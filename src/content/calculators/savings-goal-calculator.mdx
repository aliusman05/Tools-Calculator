Having a number in mind makes saving easier. This calculator works in two directions: it tells you how much to set aside each month to reach a goal by a certain time, or how long it will take at the monthly amount you can afford. Both versions include the interest your money earns along the way.

## How to use this calculator

1. Pick what you want to find: the **monthly saving needed**, or the **time it will take**.
2. Enter your **savings goal** and how much you have **already saved**.
3. Enter the **annual interest rate** your savings earns. Use 0% if you are keeping the money in cash.
4. For the monthly-saving option, enter the **number of years** you have. For the time option, enter the **monthly amount** you plan to save.
5. Review the chart and table to see your progress year by year.

## How it works

Savings are assumed to earn the annual rate compounded monthly, with deposits made at the end of each month. With a monthly rate **i** and **n** months:

```
Required deposit = (Goal − Current × (1 + i)^n) × i / ((1 + i)^n − 1)
```

Your existing savings grow by (1 + i)^n on their own. The deposit has to cover whatever is left, and the second part of the formula spreads that shortfall across the months, allowing for the interest each deposit earns. At zero interest it simplifies to the shortfall divided by n.

To find the time instead, the calculator solves the same relationship for n and rounds up to the next whole month.

## Worked example

You want **$50,000** in **10 years**, you have **$5,000** now, and your savings earn **4%**.

- Your existing $5,000 grows to about $7,454 by itself.
- You need to save **$288.94 a month**.
- You will deposit **$39,672.37** in total (including the $5,000 you already have), and interest adds **$10,327.63**.

Now flip it around. You want **$20,000**, you have **$1,000**, and you save **$300 a month** at 4%. You reach the goal after **57 months** (4 years 9 months), having deposited **$18,100** with **$1,906.95** of interest.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Automate the deposit.** A transfer set up on payday is the most reliable way to hit a monthly target.
- **Keep the goal in today's money.** If you will need the money in many years, increase the target to allow for rising prices.
- **Do not count on high returns.** Using an optimistic rate makes the required deposit look smaller than it should be.
- **Revisit your plan.** If you fall behind, enter your current balance again and see the new monthly amount needed.
- **Separate goals.** An emergency fund and a house deposit have different timelines, so run each one on its own.
