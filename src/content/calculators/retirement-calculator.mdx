Saving for retirement is a long game, and small changes today compound into large differences decades from now. This calculator projects the value of your 401(k) or similar retirement account at your chosen retirement age, including your contributions, your employer's match and investment growth. It also shows what that balance might be worth in today's dollars and what a sample withdrawal rate could provide.

## How to use this calculator

1. Enter your **current age** and the **age you plan to retire**.
2. Add your **current retirement savings** and your **annual salary**.
3. Set your **contribution** as a percentage of salary, then the **employer match** rate and the salary percentage it applies to.
4. Review the **assumptions**: expected return, salary growth, inflation and withdrawal rate. The defaults are reasonable starting points, but try a pessimistic set too.
5. Read the projected balance and the chart, then change one input at a time to see what moves the result most.

## How it works

The projection runs month by month. Each month your balance earns a share of the yearly return, and your contribution and your employer's match are added at the end of the month:

```
Balance(next) = Balance × (1 + monthly return) + your contribution + employer match
```

- Your contribution is your **salary ÷ 12 × your percentage**.
- The match is your **salary ÷ 12 × the smaller of your percentage and the match limit × the match rate**.
- Your salary increases once a year by the growth rate you enter.
- The monthly return is the yearly return converted to its monthly equivalent, so twelve months compound to exactly the annual figure.

To express the final balance in today's dollars, it is divided by (1 + inflation) raised to the number of years. The income illustration multiplies the balance by the withdrawal rate.

## Worked example

You are **35**, plan to retire at **65**, have **$50,000** saved and earn **$85,000**. You contribute **10%**, your employer matches **50% up to 6%**, your pay grows **3%** a year and your investments return **7%** a year with **2.5%** inflation.

- You contribute about **$404,391** over 30 years and your employer adds about **$121,317**.
- Investment growth adds about **$1,282,652**.
- Your projected balance at 65 is about **$1,858,360**, which is roughly **$885,960** in today's dollars.
- A 4% withdrawal would be about **$35,438** a year, or **$2,953** a month, in today's dollars.

Notice how much of the final balance comes from growth rather than deposits, and how much inflation changes the picture.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Capture the full match.** It is an immediate return on your contribution that is hard to beat anywhere else.
- **Do not assume a high return.** An optimistic rate can hide a savings gap. Test 5% as well as 7%.
- **Think in today's dollars.** A big future number can be misleading if prices will be much higher by then.
- **Increase contributions with raises.** Putting part of each raise toward retirement is painless and powerful.
- **Remember what is missing.** Taxes, fees, Social Security and healthcare costs will all affect your real retirement income.
